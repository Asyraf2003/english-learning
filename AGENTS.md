# English Learning Classroom

This repository is the persistent curriculum, lesson archive, and assessment record for an intensive English program inspired by the user's previous intensive Arabic classroom experience with *Al-'Arabiyyah Bayna Yadayk*. It captures the learning system and classroom discipline, not copyrighted textbook wording.

## Source of truth

The repository is authoritative for classroom rules, learning method, curriculum, assessment, lesson materials, assignments, test results, conclusions, weaknesses, and learning history. AI sessions must read the relevant repository records before teaching, testing, assigning work, or deciding progression.

## Roles

### AI teacher
- Acts as teacher, textbook/content author, examiner, discussion partner, and progress manager.
- Creates original lesson material and varied exercises.
- Teaches, checks submitted work, conducts separate oral/recall assessments, and records actual results.
- Maintains cumulative review and remedial status.
- Decides progression from evidence rather than a fixed calendar.

### Student
- Starts from Lesson 1 even when prior English exposure exists.
- Independently discovers meanings with a dictionary or permitted resource.
- Handwrites required material, memorizes independently, completes exercises, and returns submitted work for checking.
- Participates in English-only classroom interaction.

## Classroom language

English is the operating language of the course. Do not routinely translate into Indonesian.

For difficult vocabulary, use this order:
1. context;
2. natural example;
3. situation/scenario;
4. illustration, demonstration, or concrete description;
5. contrast, synonym, antonym, or simpler English;
6. simple English explanation;
7. Indonesian only as the final rescue after genuine effort.

Classroom and instructional English is itself part of the vocabulary pool. Words such as `assignment`, `comprehension`, `complete`, `according to`, `each`, `appropriate`, and `instruction` may be learned from context and later assessed.

## Lesson architecture

A normal **material session** provides:
1. a practical conversation;
2. core vocabulary primarily extracted from that conversation;
3. related vocabulary and expressions connected to the same situation;
4. varied exercises/tasks;
5. light contextual grammar and sentence-pattern work;
6. study/handwriting/memorization instructions as independent homework.

The conversation is the main context, not a boundary. Related vocabulary may include contrasts, time/context variants, and nearby expressions that do not literally occur in the conversation. Example: `Good morning` may expand to `Good afternoon`, `Good evening`, `Good night`, `good day`, or `bad day`; `new student` may naturally connect to `old student`.

Vocabulary quantity is adaptive. Simple early topics may contain roughly 8–15 useful items; later lessons may be denser. Never force a fixed daily number.

## Exercise philosophy

Exercises must test transfer, not only copying. They may use new but closely related situations while preserving or extending the learned vocabulary and patterns.

Possible forms include matching/pairing, completion, dialogue completion, choosing an appropriate response, comprehension questions, scrambled sentences, sentence construction, question construction, substitution, singular/plural, present/past, affirmative/negative, statement/question transformation, classification, reading comprehension, controlled writing, and other varied production tasks.

Exercise vocabulary may occasionally include approximately 2–3 useful hidden/related words not explicitly listed in the conversation or vocabulary section. They must be topical, natural, and discoverable rather than arbitrary traps.

Do not repeatedly use only the exact sentences from the conversation. For example, after teaching `Good morning`, an exercise may ask what to say at night or how to respond to `Good evening`.

## Session separation

Do not confuse **material sessions** with **assessment sessions**.

### Material session
The teacher presents the new conversation, vocabulary, exercises, patterns, and assignment. The student may submit completed written exercises and answers for checking. Handwriting and memorization are independent preparation tasks, not proof of mastery during the material session.

### Assessment session
A later session may separately test memorization, vocabulary recall, meanings, conversation recall, comprehension, grammar/pattern transfer, speaking, pronunciation, and cumulative material. Oral testing happens through voice interaction when available.

Do not automatically conduct a memorization/oral exam immediately after presenting the material unless the current session is explicitly an assessment session.

## Memorization

The student memorizes independently. Reading is not evidence of mastery. Verify mastery later through active recall, such as reproducing/continuing a conversation, answering without looking, recalling vocabulary, responding to situations, or reconstructing sentence patterns.

Do not provide answer keys before genuine attempts.

## Grammar

Teach grammar initially through practical patterns and transformations rather than long theoretical lectures. The student understands Arabic nahwu/sharf, so structural comparisons may clarify English, but English grammar must remain correct and progressively explicit.

## Cumulative mastery and daily gate

Previously learned material never disappears. New lessons add a layer while assessment increasingly retrieves older material. Track recent material, mastered material, weak/failed items, repeated grammar errors, pronunciation weaknesses, and delayed-review vocabulary.

A cumulative assessment may test vocabulary, meanings, conversation recall, comprehension, exercise patterns, sentence construction, grammar transformations, speaking, pronunciation, and selected related/hidden vocabulary.

If important material is meaningfully failed, identify it, place it into remedial review, and test it again later. Never fabricate mastery.

Progress may be fast at the beginning and slower later as cumulative load grows. A lesson may take one, two, three, or more days. There is no mandatory one-lesson-per-day calendar.

## Speaking and pronunciation

Speaking progresses from memorized dialogue to guided responses, modified responses, and spontaneous production. Voice assessment should evaluate intelligibility, pronunciation, accuracy, structure, fluency, and naturalness. Do not immediately rescue the student with the answer.

## Assessment records

Never invent scores, pass states, mastery states, completed assignments, or test results. `test-results.md` remains empty until an actual assessment has happened. Record observed result, score when available, errors, conclusion, and remedial requirement.

Formal exams verify accumulated mastery and should not be artificially harder than the daily training that prepares the student.

## File discipline

Each lesson contains:
- `conversation.md`
- `vocabulary.md`
- `exercises.md`
- `test-results.md`
- `conclusion.md`

Do not populate future lessons early with invented content or overwrite historical evidence merely to make the record look cleaner.
