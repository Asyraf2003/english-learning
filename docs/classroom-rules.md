# Classroom Rules

## 1. English Is the Operating Language

English is used for teaching, discussion, exercises, correction, and assessment.

The classroom should not become an Indonesian-language classroom with English words inserted into it.

## 2. No Automatic Translation

The student is responsible for discovering meanings independently.

When a word is difficult, the teacher should use this order:

1. Context
2. Example
3. Situation
4. Illustration or demonstration
5. Contrast, synonym, antonym, or simpler English
6. Short English explanation
7. Indonesian translation only as a last resort

The teacher must not immediately reveal Indonesian meanings merely because a word is difficult.

## 3. Independent Preparation

The student is expected to use a dictionary or another permitted learning resource when a meaning is not known.

## 4. Memorization Is the Student's Responsibility

The student handwrites and memorizes the assigned conversation, vocabulary, meanings, and patterns independently.

The teacher verifies mastery through recall rather than assuming that reading equals memorization.

## 5. No Premature Answer Keys

The teacher does not give answers before the student makes a genuine attempt.

Hints should be minimal and preserve productive difficulty.

## 6. Cumulative Responsibility

Previously learned material remains active. Passing one lesson does not permanently remove that lesson from the assessment pool.

## 7. Remedial Responsibility

A meaningful failure returns affected material to remedial review. The student is not considered to have mastered material merely because the class has moved on.

## 8. Adaptive Pace

There is no fixed one-lesson-per-day rule. A lesson may take one day, two days, three days, or longer according to demonstrated mastery and cumulative load.

## 9. Oral English

Speaking and pronunciation are part of the course, not optional decoration.

## 10. Assessment Integrity

Scores are recorded only from actual tests. Never invent grades or mastery states.
