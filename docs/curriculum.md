# Curriculum

## Starting Point

Begin at Lesson 1 even when the student already knows some English.

The first lessons provide calibration, habit formation, and foundation building. Existing exposure may allow rapid progression through simple material, but progression still depends on demonstrated mastery.

## Topic Progression

The curriculum moves from high-frequency everyday English toward progressively less frequent and more demanding language.

### Early topics

- greetings;
- introductions;
- names and personal information;
- family;
- food and drinks;
- home;
- school and study;
- daily routines;
- friends;
- shopping;
- time and dates;
- weather;
- simple transport.

### Middle topics

- markets;
- terminals and travel;
- restaurants;
- banks and services;
- campus life;
- work situations;
- common public services;
- appointments;
- practical problem solving.

### Later topics

- offices;
- hospitals;
- administration;
- media;
- education;
- technology;
- society;
- economics;
- environment;
- abstract discussion;
- argumentation;
- academic and professional communication.

The program does not rush into rare vocabulary early. Everyday English becomes stable first.

## Adaptive Pacing

There is no rigid one-lesson-per-day rule.

A very simple lesson may be completed in one day when the student demonstrates strong mastery. A denser lesson may take two days, three days, or longer.

The teacher decides progression from evidence, not from a calendar.

## Controlled New Load

The quantity of new material should remain reasonably controlled. Do not create an arbitrary vocabulary flood.

Difficulty increases mainly because:

`new material + cumulative retrieval responsibility + broader contexts + more independent production`

The student's daily burden therefore grows even when the amount of newly introduced vocabulary remains moderate.

## Cumulative Curriculum

Once a lesson is learned, it remains part of the long-term retrieval pool.

Older material may return through vocabulary recall, conversation recall, comprehension, sentence construction, grammar transformations, speaking prompts, pronunciation checks, and mixed review.

Weak material returns more frequently. Strong material returns with wider spacing.

## Long-Term Goal

Foundation → everyday communication → functional real-world English → professional English → academic English → advanced English → IELTS preparation.

IELTS preparation builds on established language ability rather than replacing the core curriculum from the beginning.
